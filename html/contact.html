What Happens Next

A transparent process, not a guaranteed one.

We can't promise a specific outcome or a fixed timeline, but we can tell you exactly what we'll do with what you send.

  1. A human reads it

    Your message is read by one of the people who'd actually be involved in the work — not a triage system.

  2. We reply with a question or a no

    Usually the reply is a clarifying question. Sometimes it's a straight answer that we're not the right fit.

  3. A short conversation

    If there's something worth exploring, we'll suggest a short call to understand the problem properly.

  4. A written shape, if it fits

    If it looks like a good match, we'll put the proposed engagement in writing — scope, approach, and what we wouldn't do.

A Thoughtful First Meeting

The first conversation is a working session, not a pitch.

We don't come with a deck. We come with questions. If we understand the problem properly, the useful next step usually becomes obvious to everyone in the room.

  • You do most of the talking

    We're trying to understand your context, not sell you ours.

  • We'll say if it's not a fit

    Early honesty saves everyone weeks. We're comfortable saying no.

  • You leave with something useful

    Even if we don't work together, we'll share what we'd do in your position.

What We Don't Do

Clear boundaries make better work.

Knowing what we won't take on is as useful as knowing what we will. It keeps the work honest and the promises small enough to keep.

  • We don't take work we can't do well

    If the problem is outside what we're genuinely good at, we'll say so rather than stretch.

  • We don't run open-ended retainers

    Ongoing work is welcome — but it needs a clear problem to be working on, not just hours to fill.

  • We don't promise timelines before we understand

    A date quoted without context is a guess dressed up as a commitment.

  • We don't talk about client work publicly

    No logos, no case studies, no details shared without explicit permission.

Our Approach to Communication

A few expectations we set early.

Most frustration in working relationships comes from unspoken expectations. These are ours, stated plainly, so you can decide whether they match how you like to work.

  • Written first

    Decisions, questions, and context go in writing. It's slower to produce and much faster to rely on.

  • Direct, without ceremony

    We say what we think, including when we're unsure. You don't need to read between the lines.

  • Regular, not noisy

    Enough contact to stay aligned, not so much that it becomes the work itself.

  • Small enough to be honest

    No account layer, no relayed messages. You talk to the people doing the work.
